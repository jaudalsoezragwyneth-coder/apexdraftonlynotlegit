R.A.P.T.O.R. GT

"Baby Raptor" | Reinforced Aerodynamic Performance Touring Rim

A full-face, high-speed sport-touring helmet built on an aerodynamic composite shell. Styled in a matte dark charcoal and tactical tan finish with high-visibility orange accents. Built for maximum protection, precision stability, and modern functionality for the demanding rider.

Material Selection & Architecture

A. Outer Shell Composite

  • Exterior Layer (1.00 mm): Epoxy UV Gelcoat Finish layered over 3 layers of woven E-glass & S-glass hybrid reinforcement in an epoxy matrix. Purpose: Balances cost and high mechanical performance. Improves impact resistance.
  • Intermediate Reinforcement (0.90 mm): 2 layers of woven S-Fiberglass cloth. Purpose: High abrasion resistance; prevents cracking during heavy impacts.
  • Inner Layer (1.20 mm): 8 layers of high-density Aramid (Kevlar) hybrid weave. Purpose: Core energy absorption and structural integrity.
  • Resin Matrix: Epoxy resin (60% fiber / 40% resin by weight).

B. Impact Absorbing Layer

  • Crown (Top): High-density EPS — 40 mm (Density: 50 g/L)
  • Forehead: High-density EPS (Density: 70 g/L)
  • Temples: Medium Density EPS — 40 mm (Density: 40 g/L)
  • Rim: Soft EPS — 20 mm (Density: 35 g/L)
  • Purpose: Multi-density EPS zones are engineered specifically to absorb kinetic energy during collisions by crushing at targeted rates, protecting specific cranial sectors.

C & D. Comfort Isolation & Restraint

  • Comfort Padding: Crown (15mm Open-cell PU + 3D Air Mesh), Cheek/Jaw (30mm High-density PU), Neck (20mm Dense PU + Neoprene sweat barrier). Purpose: PU foam deforms slowly turning energy into heat. Air mesh aids breathability. Neoprene and 1.2mm moisture-wicking polyester limit sweat.
  • Chin Strap: 1.6 mm High-Tenacity Woven Polyester (>15kN tensile strength) with 4.0mm PU foam sleeves. Purpose: HTWP holds the helmet securely during catastrophic events.
  • Hardware: Marine-grade 316 Stainless Double D-Rings, cold-pressed anchor plates, solid rivets, and PC snap button.

E & F. Visor & Aerodynamics

  • Ventilation System: Aggressive Chin Vent, Crown Ports, Internal Air Grooves, and Rear Exhaust Ports.
  • Visor Structure: Optical Class 1 Polycarbonate (PC) with a medical-grade silicone perimeter gasket. Purpose: Provides maximum optical clarity, shielding debris while the gasket seals out dust/water.
  • Inner Lens: Hydrophilic Triacetate Cellulose (TAC) insert (Pinlock 120 standard). Purpose: Eliminates visor fogging from rider respiration.
  • Visor Tech: Sun-reactive Spirooxazine photochromic dye matrix, coated in Hydrophobic Fluoro-silane and Polysiloxane UV hard-coat.

Next-Gen Safety Features

Smart Brake Light

Integrated accelerometer-triggered rear LED light bar. Driven by an internal 3-axis MEMS sensor, it illuminates brilliantly during sudden deceleration to warn trailing vehicles without physical brake line connections.

Waveguide HUD

An integrated optical waveguide Heads-Up Display projecting crucial turn-by-turn navigation and speed metrics seamlessly into the lower visor periphery.

Crash Detection & eCall

Equipped with a tri-axial high-G impact detector (>100G threshold). Upon severe impact detection, it automatically transmits post-impact telemetry and precise GPS coordinates to emergency services via LTE.

Fatigue Monitoring

Contact photoplethysmography (PPG) pulse-rate sensors are embedded discreetly in the cheek pad fabric. These monitor Heart Rate Variability (HRV) in real-time to alert the rider against micro-sleeps and drowsiness.

Safety Test Simulation & Physics Explanation

Linear Momentum and Impulse

Momentum is the quantity of motion an object possesses, defined as the product of its mass and velocity ($p = mv$) (Urone & Hinrichs, 2020b). In a crash, a rider's head possesses significant momentum. Upon impact, this momentum must be reduced to zero. The impulse-momentum theorem ($F \Delta t = \Delta p$) dictates that the change in momentum ($\Delta p$) is equal to the average force ($F$) multiplied by the time duration of the impact ($\Delta t$). The R.A.P.T.O.R. GT's multi-density EPS core is designed to crush progressively, effectively prolonging the collision time ($\Delta t$). By increasing the time it takes for the head to stop, the peak force ($F$) transmitted to the skull is drastically reduced (Urone & Hinrichs, 2020b).

Newton's Second Law and Deceleration

According to Newton’s Second Law, the net force acting on an object is proportional to its mass and acceleration ($F = ma$) (Admin, 2024). When a head strikes a rigid surface, it undergoes a massive, instantaneous deceleration ($a$). The helmet's highly engineered composite padding mitigates this violent change. By crushing at controlled rates, the EPS foam makes this deceleration less sudden. A lower deceleration directly results in a lower net force experienced by the brain, preventing severe traumatic brain injuries.

Kinetic Energy and the Work-Energy Theorem

Impact absorption is the conversion of kinetic energy into material deformation during a crash (Brooks, 2026b). A moving rider possesses kinetic energy calculated as $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. To bring the rider's head to a safe stop, the helmet must dissipate this energy by doing physical work ($W = Fd$, where $d$ is the deformation distance). The Kevlar and S-glass outer shell first disperses the localized impact over a wider surface area. Then, the underlying EPS matrix takes in and dissipates the kinetic energy by permanently deforming (crushing). This irreversible plastic deformation converts the lethal kinetic energy into harmless heat and mechanical destruction, sacrificing the helmet to save the rider.

Rotational Force Deflection

Most real-world impacts occur at oblique angles, introducing rotational acceleration—a primary cause of severe concussions. The aerodynamic, smooth profile of the R.A.P.T.O.R. GT’s UV gelcoat finish is engineered to slide across abrasive surfaces like asphalt. This reduces friction and prevents the helmet from "catching," thereby minimizing the transfer of angular momentum and rotational sheer forces to the rider's neck and brain.
